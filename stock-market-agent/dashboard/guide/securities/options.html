Options (Calls & Puts)

Derivative

A contract giving you the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell a stock at a fixed price before a set date.

How it works

A call gives you the right to buy a stock at a set "strike" price before expiry — worth buying if you think the stock will rise above that strike. A put gives you the right to sell at the strike — worth buying if you think the stock will fall below it. You pay a premium upfront, priced (using a model like Black-Scholes) off the stock's price, the strike, time left, and the stock's own volatility; the more volatile the stock, the pricier the option.

Why it's leveraged

A small move in the underlying stock can mean a much larger percentage move in the option's value, because you're only paying for the right to the move, not the full share price. That cuts both ways: if the stock never reaches your strike, the option can expire completely worthless, and you lose the whole premium — a 100% loss is a normal, not a rare, outcome here.
