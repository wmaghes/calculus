Mutual Funds

Diversified

A pooled investment vehicle much like an ETF, but priced once a day instead of trading live all day.

How it works

Like an ETF, a mutual fund pools many investors' money into a diversified basket of holdings. The key difference: you buy and sell at the fund's Net Asset Value (NAV), calculated just once after the market closes each day — not a live, continuously-updating price. That makes it less convenient for active trading but no less diversified.

Variations on this instrument

Index mutual fund — the mutual-fund equivalent of a broad index ETF: passively tracks a benchmark, very low fees.
Actively managed fund — a professional manager picks and changes holdings trying to beat the market, usually for a noticeably higher fee; most fail to beat their index after fees over long periods.
Balanced / income fund — mixes bonds and dividend stocks in one fund for a steadier, lower-volatility ride than an all-stock fund.

Risk

Same diversification benefit as an ETF; an actively managed fund adds the manager's own strategy and judgment as an extra variable, for better or worse, on top of the market's own moves.
