Short Selling

Bearish strategy

Borrowing shares to sell now, betting the price falls so you can buy them back cheaper later.

How it works

You borrow shares you don't own (typically from your broker) and sell them at today's price, pocketing the proceeds. Later, you "cover" the position by buying the same number of shares back and returning them. If the price fell in between, you profit the difference; if it rose, you lose the difference. Opening a short requires margin (collateral) and costs an ongoing borrow fee for as long as the position stays open.

Why the risk is asymmetric

Buying a stock outright, the most you can ever lose is 100% of what you paid — a stock can't fall below zero. Shorting flips that: a stock can rise without any upper limit, so a short position's potential loss is theoretically unlimited. This is exactly why short sellers watch margin requirements so closely, and why a short squeeze (a sharp rally forcing short sellers to buy back at any price) can be so damaging.
